Substance Abuse Testing Policy

1. About Policy

1.1. Objective

IPAK maintains a strict policy against the use of unlawful/prohibited drugs while at work or on company premises.

1.2. Scope

This policy is applicable to all the employees of International Packaging Films Ltd.

1.3. Exception

All the exceptions of the policy will be approved by the Head of HR.

1.4. Frequency of Review

This policy will be reviewed on need basis if any changes are required.

1.5. Authority

The Company reserves the right to amend this policy or withdraw it in full at any time without assigning any reason.

1.6. Applicability

This policy is applicable with effect from Jan 01, 2026.

2. Policy Guidelines

2.1. Policy Details

  1. To facilitate the administration and enforcement of this policy, IPAK may require or request employees to submit drug or substance abuse testing under certain circumstances.
  2. In cases where an employee's manager has reasonable suspicion to believe that the employee possesses or is under the influence of drugs, substance or drug screening may be ordered.
  3. HOD will inform HR Department for such related matters to proceed with the required actions according to the policy.
  4. This suspicion must be based on any objective symptoms, such as factors related to the employee's appearance, behavior, speech and/or other factors.
  5. If an employee is on medically prescribed medication, it is the employee's responsibility to advise his/her supervisor of this fact before he or she reports to work.
  6. Testing may also be required if an employee is found to be in possession of physical substances that could relate to the use of an illicit drug.
  7. Such evidence includes, but is not limited to drugs, drug paraphernalia or alcohol.
  8. Possession of drugs or alcohol on IPAK's property is prohibited whether or not it is determined that the employee also used such substances.

2.2. Grading Disciplinary Action

  1. The Management shall examine any such findings and decide which action should be taken against the accused.
  2. In case of any criminal breach, the company reserves the right to refer the matter to state authorities for an appropriate action in accordance with law.

Disciplinary Matters Policy

1. About Policy

1.1. Objective

The objective of the policy is to describe the standards of discipline/conduct to be observed by the employees and lays down the process to be followed to ensure a fair and transparent process of handling in-discipline/misconduct cases.

1.2. Scope

This policy is applicable to all the employees of International Packaging Films Ltd.

1.3. Exception

All the exceptions of the policy will be approved by the Head of HR.

1.4. Frequency of Review

This policy will be reviewed on need basis if any changes are required.

1.5. Authority

The Company reserves the right to amend this policy or withdraw it in full at any time without assigning any reason.

1.6. Applicability

This policy is applicable with effect from Jan 01, 2026.

2. Policy Guidelines

2.1. Discipline

Standards of conduct have been embodied in the:

  1. IPAK Code of Conduct
  2. Applicable laws or government regulations
  3. IPAK Policy Manuals & SOPs
  4. Any other policy of IPAK

2.2. Examples Of Misconduct

  1. Willful in-subordination or disobedience to any lawful and reasonable directions of a Line Manager or Management.
  2. Theft, Fraud, or dishonesty in connection IPAK's business or property.
  3. Taking or giving bribes or any illegal gratification.
  4. Riotous or disorderly behavior during working hours.
  5. Malingering/faking illness or other incapability to avoid duty or work.
  6. Harassment of other employees.
  7. Habitual absence without leave.
  8. Habitual late attendance.
  9. Habitual breach of any law applicable to the establishment.
  10. Habitual negligence or neglect of work.
Note: Cases of sexual Harassment will be dealt with as defined in the law, "The Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010".

2.3. Disciplinary Matters Procedure

  1. When a Line Manager/HR Manager receives report about the alleged misconduct, he/she shall examine the situation for authenticity of facts within 48 hours.
  2. If the allegation amounts to misconduct, then "Show Cause Notice" shall be issued by HR Department.
  3. The accused should be given 3 calendar days (72 hours) to submit his/her written explanation.
  4. The show cause shall be served personally upon the employee concerned.
  5. In case the explanation is not found satisfactory, formal inquiry into the matter would be initiated.
  6. Head of HR after consultation with the Management, would finalize whether such inquiry would be conducted "on job" or "suspend from services till facts are clear".
  7. The Inquiry Officer shall write down all the proceedings and get the signature of the accused.
  8. The inquiry officer shall, after giving due opportunity of hearing and cross examination, submit his/her findings/report to the Management.

2.4. Disciplinary Action

The Management shall examine the inquiry findings and decide which action should be taken:

  1. Warning / Reprimand
  2. Demotion to a lower position
  3. Stoppage of one-year increment
  4. Seizure/reduction of perks & benefits
  5. Dismissal from service

2.5. Criminal Breach

In case of any criminal breach, the company reserves the right to refer the matter to state authorities.

2.6. Resolution

The disciplinary action may be in writing and given to the accused. If the allegations are not proven, the matter will be dropped.

2. Policy Guidelines

2.1. Rules & Requirements

  1. Email is a business communication tool and users are obliged to use this tool in a responsible, effective, and lawful manner.
  2. The following rules must be strictly adhered to. It is prohibited to:
    • Send or forward emails containing libelous, defamatory, offensive, racist or obscene remarks.
    • Forward or copy a message containing confidential or copyrighted information without prior approval.
    • Send unsolicited email messages or forge email messages.
    • Send email messages using another person's email account.

2.2. Writing Emails

  1. Write well-structured emails and use short, descriptive subjects.
  2. IPAK email style is informal. Signatures must include your name, job title and company name.
  3. Spell check all mails prior to transmission.
  4. Do not send unnecessary attachments. Compress attachments larger than 200K before sending.
  5. Do not write emails in capitals.
  6. Only mark emails as important if they really are important.

2.3. Personal Use

The company's email system is meant for business use only. All messages distributed via the company's email system are IPAK's property.

2.4. System Monitoring

Users of the company's email system expressly waive any right of privacy. IPAK may monitor emails without prior notification.

2.5. Declaration

Employees are required to sign a copy of the Email Policy declaring that they have read, understood, and will comply with the guidelines.

Anti-Harassment Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Policy Details

  1. International Packaging Films Ltd. is committed to providing an environment that is free from discrimination and harassment.
  2. In keeping with this commitment, International Packaging Films Ltd. maintains a strict policy prohibiting all forms of harassment, including sexual harassment and harassment based on race, national or ethnic origin, gender, religious beliefs, age, marital status, sexual orientation, or disability.
  3. This policy extends to all employees. This includes all employees, temporary employees & other professional service providers.
  4. Furthermore, it prohibits harassment in any form including verbal, non-verbal and physical harassment.

1.2. Types of Harassment

Harassment is an unwelcoming conduct of an individual which makes the other person feel offended, humiliated and/or intimidated. This also includes situations which create an environment which is hostile, intimidating or humiliating for the recipient.

a. Physical Harassment

  • A situation where physical contact is involved like patting, pinching, stroking, fondling or inappropriate touching
  • Physical violence, including assault
  • The use of job-related threats or rewards to solicit sexual favors

b. Verbal Harassment

  • Comments on appearance, age, private life etc.
  • Sexual comments, stories, and jokes
  • Repeated and unwanted social invitation like dating etc.
  • Condescending or paternalistic remarks
  • Sending sexually explicit messages by phone or email

c. Non-Verbal Harassment

  • Display of sexually explicit or suggestive material
  • Nonverbal gestures and mocking
  • Whistling and leering

1.3. Explanation

Harassment may include, but is not limited to, making unwanted sexual advances and requests for favors where either:

  1. Submission to such conduct is made an explicit or implicit term or condition of employment.
  2. Submission to or rejection of such conduct by an individual is used as the basis for employment decisions affecting such individual; or
  3. Such conduct has the purpose or effect of substantially interfering with an individual’s work performance or creating an intimidating, hostile or offensive working environment.

1.4. Harassment Grievance Handling Procedure

  1. Any employee who believes he or she has been harassed or physically threatened or harmed should promptly report in writing, the facts of the incident or incidents and the names of the individuals involved to the Human Resources Department.
  2. It is the responsibility of each employee to immediately report any violation or suspected violation of this policy to Human Resource Department.
  3. Additionally, supervisors and managers should immediately report any incidents of harassment to Human Resource Department.
  4. The Human Resources Department will promptly, thoroughly, impartially, and confidentially investigate any complaints of harassment.
  5. Appropriate corrective action, including disciplinary action, will be taken when warranted.
  6. All harassment grievances will be handled according to the procedure defined in “IPAK Grievance Handling Policy” & “Disciplinary Matters Policy”.

1.5. Disciplinary Measures

  1. Any employee engaged in harassment activity, may face disciplinary action.
  2. Consequences could range from reprimand to termination of employment, depending on the severity of the violation, and will be determined on a case-by-case basis.

Employee Resignation Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Procedure

  1. The employee can submit the resignation to the Line Manager or HOD clearly mentioning the last working day.
  2. HOD/Line manager will share the resignation of the resigned employee with HR within 48 hours.
  3. Final settlement will be proceeded only if employee submits the complete final clearance documentation.
  4. HR will proceed with separation process as per the SOP of separation/final settlement.

1.2. Notice Period for Resignation / Termination

  • Notice period for resignations is divided in two categories, generally for permanent (Confirmed) employees, notice period will be of 30 days and for employees on the probation and contract, it will be of 7 days (or as contract).
  • If an employee resigns and leaves without serving the required notice period, shall pay the equivalent salary-in-lieu of such a notice period (un-served) based on the last drawn gross salary.
  • The working day on which the resignation is submitted shall be included in the notice period.
    1. If an employee submits the resignation while on leave the notice period will start on the day of resumption of duty i.e., the tenure spent on leave will not be considered in the notice period service.

1.3. Specific Guidelines for Experience Letter

  • No experience letter will be issued if an employee is terminated due to gross misconduct. As a special case, such letter will be issue on recommendation of HOD and approved by Head of HR.
    1. All Experience letters will be signed by Head of HR after review of personal file and records.

1.4. Waiver of Notice Period for Resignation/Termination

  • The notice period to be served by an employee who resigns may be waived, fully or partially.
  • Where the Company agrees to a full waiver or part thereof, employee whose obligation is to serve notice, need not pay to the salary in lieu of the notice period waived.
  • Head of HR may consider waiving employee’s notice period under the following exceptional circumstances:
    • On medical grounds at the advice of registered medical practitioner e.g., disability (fully or partially), etc.
    • On compassionate grounds: e.g., death of employee or death of a family member due to which a permanent relief from duty by the employee is necessary to attend the family problems, etc.
    • In all other situations, the request for waiver of notice shall be reviewed by the Head of HR, based on the individual merit(s) of the case and recommendation from the HOD.

1.5. Leave During Notice Period

  1. Resigned employee serving notice period can avail casual and sick leaves only as per the available quota.
  2. Last working day would be the day when employee was present physically in office premises.
  3. If an employee resigns and the Last Working Day (LWD) falls on a gazetted holiday or an off day, the LWD will be considered as the working day immediately preceding the holiday/off day. The final settlement will still be calculated based on the notice period as stipulated up to the original LWD; however, no deduction will be made in the final settlement on account of the LWD adjustment or any perceived shortfall in the notice period.

Anti-Nepotism Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. General Rules

  1. The Company is committed to provide employment and advancement of the employees based solely on qualification and merit.
  2. Being related by blood or marriage to a current employee will not impact a candidate’s chances at being offered a position at IPAK if they are otherwise suitable for the job. However, nepotism can cause several workplace issues including perceived or real favoritism, conflicts of interest, family conflicts, reduced productivity, and scheduling conflicts.
  3. For the purposes of this policy, “family” and “a relative” refers to anyone related to an employee by blood, marriage, or other legal relationship, including:
    First LevelSecond LevelThird Level
    SpousesFirst CousinsDistant Cousins
    ChildrenNieces/NephewsDistant Nieces/Nephews
    ParentsIn-Laws
    Siblings
    Grandparents/Grandchildren
    Aunts/Uncles
    Adoptive Family Members
  4. To avoid potential favoritism, conflicts of interest and workplace disruptions, IPAK does not allow employee family members (as defined above).
  5. The following rules will apply:
    • First level relation cannot be hired across the organization.
    • Second level & third level relation cannot work under the same manager
    • Second level & third level relation cannot work in a reporter/supervisor relationship (chain of command)
    • Second level & third level relation cannot hire or supervise one another
    • Second level & third level relation cannot evaluate, audit, or review one another’s work
  6. Human Resources Department will coordinate with the employee’s direct supervisor to develop appropriate plans to ensure that two family members are not in the same chain of command.
  7. If the situation is not resolved, then the Human Resources Department will affect the transfer of either employee to ensure no potential conflict under this policy.
  8. HOD in the chain of command of the two family members will agree on and implement the appropriate action in conjunction with Human Resource Department.
  9. Human Resources shall investigate reports of nepotism and take appropriate steps after consultation with the Management.
  10. Employees are required to disclose changes in their personal situations which may be covered by this procedure.
  11. Supervisors or Managers may inquire about the family relationship between employees to determine the appropriateness of the working relationship under this policy.
  12. If two employees become relatives while they are employed at IPAK and they work in any of the prohibited scenarios, one of them must either be transferred or have their tasks temporarily reassigned to another employee. The employees may decide amongst themselves which will be moved and if they cannot come to an agreement, IPAK will decide for them. If you are in this situation and need help in deciding, HR and the HOD will be able to offer support.
  13. If an employee is a relative of a candidate for an open position, they will be temporarily removed from their hiring duties.

1.2. Disciplinary Action

  1. Any employee found to violate this policy, including failure to disclose a family relationship, may face disciplinary action.
  2. Consequences could range from reprimand to termination of employment, depending on the severity of the violation, and will be determined on a case-by-case basis.

Leave Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Introduction

  1. IPAK recognizes the importance of Work Life Balance and to facilitate the employee, leaves are given to the employees to maintain that balance.
  2. Leave shall not be claimed by any employee as a matter of right.
  3. The granting of leave shall be subject to the exigencies of the Company business.
  4. The submission of an application shall not be deemed to be valid until formally sanctioned by the Competent Authority.

1.2. General Rules

  1. Head of Department / Competent Authority is authorized to approve all kind of leaves of employees within their respective department.
  2. Leave Application other than Sick & Casual Leave (In case of emergency) will be submitted to HR Department with prior approval of Head of Department.
  3. Without prior approval of immediate Supervisor and respective HOD, the leave cannot be availed.
  4. Any leave without approval will be considered as Absent.
  5. The approved leaves can be canceled by HOD if there is Business need.
  6. Multiple types of the leaves cannot be combined, however in special circumstances, multiple type of leaves can be combined with the approval of the Head of HR.

1.3. Types of Leave

The types of leave available to employees are:

  1. Annual leave
  2. Casual leave
  3. Sick leave
  4. Special Sick Leave
  5. Maternity leave
  6. Paternity Leave
  7. Hajj Leave
  8. Leave without pay
  9. Compensatory Leave (CPL)
  10. Compassionate Leave

1.4. Annual Leave

  1. Every permanent employee will be eligible for Annual Leave upon confirmation of service. However, the period of probation shall, in case of confirmed employees, be included in the period of service when computing entitlement of the annual leave.
  2. An unconfirmed employee, during his period of probation, shall not be entitled to avail annual leave.
  3. All eligible employees will be entitled to 15 (Fifteen) Working Days Annual Leaves.
  4. Full salary and allowances shall be admissible during annual leave.
  5. Any permanent employee entitled to avail earned leave may apply for it as per defined SOP prior to the proposed date of proceeding on leave.
  6. The application for earned leave shall be approved by Head of Department / Competent Authority.
  7. When proper notice has been received and approval granted, the employee may be allowed to proceed on leave from the date applied for.
  8. The Company reserves the right, to suspend or refuse earned leave, if the needs of the Company so demand.
  9. The Company may at its discretion, require the employee to make such reasonable adjustment in the proposed period of leave as may be mutually agreed.
  10. Balance of Annual Leaves over 30 days will be encashed at the end of calendar year.
  11. Annual Leaves will be encashed based on the last drawn Basic Salary.

1.5. Casual Leave

  1. Casual leave may be granted up to a maximum of 10 (ten) working days in each year.
  2. The sanctioning authority may allow casual leave of up to 3 (three) consecutive days at any one time.
  3. Employees can avail the casual leaves in the form of half-day leaves (up to 4 hours) or short leaves (up to 2 hours).
  4. Half day leave of more than four (4) hours will be treated as a full day leave, while a short leave of more than two (2) hours and less than four or equal to four (4) will be treated as half day leave.
  5. Employees may avail short leave of maximum 02 hours due to any personal issue. These 02 hours short leave duration will be deducted from his/her casual leave balance only.
  6. If employee takes short leave and doesn’t return after 02 hours, then half day leave shall be deducted from his/her leave balance. If his/her leave’s duration exceeds 04 hours, then full day leave shall be deducted from leave balance.
  7. Casual leave will only be allowed with the prior approval of the line Manager and approval will be submitted to HR before proceeding to the leave.
  8. In case of any emergency where employee must take the day off, he/she will inform the Line Manager about the leave. Employee will ensure to provide approved leave application within 2 days of availed leave.

1.6. Sick Leave

  1. Sick leave shall be admissible to employees for a period not exceeding a total of 10 (ten) days in a year.
  2. Sick leave more than 3 (three) consecutive days shall have to be supported by a medical certificate.
  3. In this case if no medical certificate is provided, then it will be treated as “Leave without Pay”.
  4. Sick Leaves may be accumulated up to two years and cannot be en-cashed.
  5. Leaves for planned surgeries should be discussed by the employee with his Line Manager prior to scheduling any such leaves.
  6. For long Sick Leaves, Fitness Certificate will be furnished to HR before joining the duty. HR may restrict the employee to work without “Certified Medical Certificate”.
  7. The employee will submit the Approved Leave Application to HR as per defined SOP after joining back the duty through IPAK ESS.

1.7. Special Sick Leave

  1. Special Medical Leaves are for exceptional medical emergencies which require prolonged incapacitation.
  2. Upon exhaustion of the availed medical leave limit, employee can avail special medical leave extended up to 90 Calendar days with full pay which subject to approval of the Head of HR.
  3. In case, ailment is prolonged then the Management may allow further extension of the leaves.
  4. In further extension, the approval of leaves with or without pay is discretion of the management.

1.8. Maternity Leave

  1. Maternity leave shall be granted to expectant mothers in the regular service of the Company.
  2. The leave may be availed for a period not exceeding a total of 90 (ninety) calendar days.
  3. There is no limit to the number of maternity leave instances an employee avails during her career, however, the related medical benefit will be extended according to the medical insurance policy.
  4. In the unfortunate event of a miscarriage after 24th week of pregnancy, maternity leave rules will apply.
  5. If a miscarriage occurs before the 24th week of pregnancy, it will fall under medical leave rules.
  6. To avail maternity leave, all female employees who are expectant mothers, will have to submit a written leave application supported by a certificate signed by medical practitioner / doctor.
  7. After approval from the Line Manager, this application will be submitted to HR Department.

1.9. Paternity Leave

  1. All male employees are entitled to avail 4 calendar days of paternity leave.
  2. This leave period will require a submission of Birth Certificate containing the father’s paternity for leave entitlement.
  3. All such male employees may choose to begin their leaves on any day following the child’s birth, but must be utilized within 30 days from the date of child’s birth.
  4. Paternity Leaves will be taken in a block and not on alternative days.
  5. The employee will intimate the Line Manager in advance for the approval of such leaves.
  6. Paternity Leaves entitlement will remain same in case of Miscarriage, Premature Birth.

1.10. Hajj Leave

Hajj Balloting

Employees who will avail leaves under the Hajj Balloting Program, will be entitled to a leave of 30 calendar days.

Personal Arrangement Hajj

  • Any employee can avail the Personal Hajj Leaves only once during their employment with IPAK.
  • Employee can avail 30 calendar days Hajj leaves only once in his employment with IPAK.
  • If an employee is rehired in IPAK and he/she availed leaves in his/her last tenure with IPAK, he/she will not be entitled for Hajj Leaves.
  • All such employees who have already availed Hajj Leaves under Hajj Balloting Program will not be eligible for personal Hajj Leaves and vice versa.

1.11. Leave Without Pay

  1. Leave without Pay may be granted at the discretion of the Company in special circumstances.
  2. An employee on leave without pay shall not be entitled to claim any travel or medical or other facilities for the period during which he is on such leave.
  3. The period on authorized leave without pay is not counted in the service period of the employee.
  4. All such leaves will only be approved by the Head of HR.

1.12. Compensatory Leave (CPL)

An employee will be entitled for the CPL in line with “Attendance at Work Policy”.

1.13. Compassionate Leave

Compassionate Leave up to three calendar days may be granted on the death of immediate family member which include:

  • Parents
  • Spouse
  • Children
  • Brothers and Sister

1.14. Disclaimer

  1. All the employees are provided with the provision of leaves to manage their work life balance, however taking leave will always be subject to the approval of the competent authorities.
  2. While availing leave, business priorities will always be kept on priority.

Code of Conduct

1. Objective

  1. International Packaging Company Limited (the “IPAK”) maintains certain policies to guide its members of the Board, Senior Management, and other employees with respect to standards of conduct expected in areas wherein improper activities could damage the Company’s reputation and otherwise, result in serious adverse consequences.
  2. The Company has developed a Code of Conduct (the “Code”) to provide guidance on identifying situations that could create or have the potential to create legal and ethical problems; and subsequently provide direction in handling actual and potential conflicts of interest between their personal and professional relationships. The purpose of this policy is to establish required standards of conduct and applicable practices in a comprehensive statement.
  3. The Company shall operate in full compliance with all the applicable laws of Pakistan. The conduct of the employees shall conform to the requirements of all the applicable laws and policies, rules and regulations of Pakistan.
  4. Compliance with all the applicable laws, rules and regulations of Pakistan shall not eliminate the need to observe the corporate culture with a focus on ethics in their activities.
  5. It is important to avoid even the likelihood of any illegal or unethical behavior towards other employees. The employees shall behave in a manner that promotes the trust of other employees and general public at large and all conduct shall be well above the minimum standards of required by law.

2. Responsibility

  1. The Board of Directors are responsible to ensure that organization as a whole abide by the code of conduct of the company.
  2. The HR department shall be responsible to maintain the Code and ethics statement signed by all the employees at the time of hiring and subsequently, in each calendar year, indicating their understanding and engagement with the Code.
  3. The Head of HR shall have overall responsibility for implementing and updating the Code, adopting all relevant laws and regulations in place. He/she shall also be responsible for responding to the queries, providing clarifications, and taking appropriate action in case of non- compliance with the Code.
  4. All heads of departments shall be responsible for ensuring that the employees under their control conform to the specific terms and conditions of the Code, in letter and spirit.
  5. Every employee shall be accountable to the Company for executing his duties in a manner that conforms to the specific terms and conditions of the Code and satisfies its intent.
  6. The Company shall not condone the activities of employees who achieve results through violation of the law or unethical business dealings. This includes inter alia any payments for illegal acts, indirect contributions, rebates, and bribery.

3. Policy

Any question or uncertainty posed by the employees regarding the Code or interpretation of any legal requirements shall be addressed to the Head of HR, who shall review the question and clarify the position to the employee, and who, if necessary, shall seek appropriate legal advice. The Code provides guidance regarding the following areas:

  • Conflict of interest;
  • Improper personal benefits;
  • Financial interests in other businesses;
  • Institutional opportunities;
  • Outside employment or activities;
  • Protection and proper use of IPAK Assets;
  • Full, fair and accurate disclosure of facts;
  • Workplace harassment;
  • Sexual harassment
  • Weapons, workplace violence, swearing, drugs, alcohol and gambling;
  • Non-Smoking offices;
  • Loans and other credit facilities from financial institutions;
  • Responding to inquiries from the press and others;
  • Confidentiality;
  • Compliance with laws and the Code;
  • Rights created;
  • Disclosures of interest, affiliation or activity;
  • Whistle-blower protection; and
  • Sanction.

4. Conflict of Interest

  1. A conflict of interest shall occur when personal interests of an employee interfere, or appears to interfere in any way, with the interests of the Company. Conflicts of interest may also arise when an employee takes action or has interest, or a member of the employee’s family has interest, that may make it difficult for the employee to perform his/her duties objectively and effectively at the workplace. Employees must act with great care to avoid such conflict, at all times.
  2. If the employee becomes aware of an actual, potential, or apparent conflict of interest, he/she shall immediately bring it to the attention of their Head of Department by submitting a Conflict-of-Interest application.

5. Improper Personal Benefits

An employee shall not solicit or accept, directly or indirectly, any cash or monetary equivalents, objects of value or preferential treatment from any person or business that has or is seeking business with the Company, where doing so may influence or appear to influence an employee’s judgment.

6. Financial Interest in other Businesses

An employee shall not have an interest in any other business if that interest compromises or appears to compromise an employee’s impartiality and threatens compliance with the Company’s principles at large.

7. Outside Employment or Activities

  1. IPAK is firmly committed to complying with its legal and ethical obligations. As a result, we expect all employees, at every level within the company, to comply strictly with all legal and ethical obligations. Our philosophy can be implemented only if our employees recognize their responsibility to treat everyone in an honest and fair manner.
  2. Accordingly, an employee’s failure to fulfill his or her responsibilities may result in disciplinary action, up to and possibly including immediate termination.

8. Protection and Proper Use of IPAK Assets

  1. An employee shall be expected to protect the Company’s assets and ensure their efficient use, at all times. All Company assets shall be used for legitimate purposes, but incidental personal use may be permitted, if ancillary to the purpose of the Company.
  2. An employee shall also be prohibited from making any improper use of the property such as funds, software, e-mail systems, voice mail systems, computer networks, vehicles and facilities for personal benefit or profit.

9. Full, Fair and Accurate Disclosure of Facts

  1. The Company is committed in providing full, fair, accurate and timely disclosure in the reports, documents and communications filed with the regulatory authorities. Although certain personnel are more directly involved in the preparation of such reports, documents, and communications than others; nevertheless, the Company expects all of its employees to accept this responsibility.
  2. Accordingly, all employees shall have an ethical responsibility to provide prompt, complete and accurate information in response to any inquiry related to preparation of the Company’s public disclosure documents and public communications.
  3. Additionally, in order to ensure accurate financial reporting, the personnel who participate in the maintenance and preparation of the Company’s books, records and accounts must ensure that the transactions and events recorded therein, are done so in an accurate and complete manner, in compliance with the required accounting principles and policies of the Company.

10. Workplace Harassment

  1. The Company is committed in cultivating a diverse work environment where individual differences are appreciated and respected.
  2. The Company is committed in maintaining a safe and healthy work environment and takes all necessary health and safety precautions consistent with the current medical knowledge.
  3. Accordingly, employees shall not refuse to work with or cooperate with or withhold services from or otherwise harass or isolate a co-worker because of a known or suspected disability or disease, or because of a co-worker’ association with a person with a disability or disease.
  4. Accordingly, any form of harassment based on an individual’s race, color, sex, religion, national origin, age, or disability is a violation of this Code and shall be treated as a disciplinary matter.

11. Sexual Harassment

  1. The Company strongly condemns and prohibits any behavior on the part of its employees which constitutes any form of sexual misconduct, including sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, and sexual violence towards any other staff member, client, beneficiary, or other individual participating in the Company’s activity.
  2. A report of sexual misconduct subsequently, resulting in retaliation on part of any member of the Company is strictly prohibited and shall not be tolerated; sexual misconduct and/or retaliation of any kind shall result in termination of the employment.
  3. For the purposes of this Code, the employees shall include permanent employees, field staff or temporary contractual employees.
  4. The Company is committed in providing a work environment that is free from discrimination and unlawful harassment. Actions, words, jokes, or comments based on an individual’s sex, race, ethnicity, age, religion, or any other characteristics regulated under law of Pakistan shall not be acceptable.
  5. The Company is fully compliant with “The Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010” and “The Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Rules, 2013”.
  6. “The Code of Conduct for Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace,” as provided under the Act is annexed herewith as mentioned in the Schedule to this Code.
  7. All provisions of the said Act along with the Rules or Regulations made thereunder, shall be enforceable in the Company.

12. Weapon, Workplace Violence, Drugs, Alcohol and Gambling

  1. The Company staff shall not possess or use weapons of any kind, ammunition, gunpowder, fireworks, explosives, and other dangerous articles and substances in the Company premises.
  2. Individuals employed as contract security guards may be given authorization to carry, keep and handle firearms and ammunition.
  3. Specific classification of employees may be required to utilize dangerous articles or hazardous material to carry out their job requirements.
  4. Each department is responsible for monitoring the use of dangerous articles or hazardous materials in its areas.

13. Non-Smoking Offices

  1. Exposure to secondhand smoke increases the risk of lung cancer, heart disease and other serious illnesses.
  2. Ventilation or separating smokers from non-smokers within the same airspace does not completely prevent potentially dangerous exposure. Smoking causes discomfort and potential damage to the health of both; the smoker as well as non-smokers. Smoking may also present a safety hazard in terms of fire or explosion.
  3. This process has been developed to protect all employees and visitors from exposure to secondhand smoke and also prevent any accidents on account of fire.
  4. It is the right of all individual(s) whether they wish to smoke or not, however the Company reserves the right to determine the area(s) where smoking may be permitted, particularly in relation to its own employees, whilst at the workplace.
  5. The Company is committed in making its buildings completely smoking-free for the protection of its staff and visitors. Specifically, smoking is not permitted in any office building or within 5 meters of the entrance to the premises and open windows.
  6. As with any other request for time off, an employee wishing to take a break from work to smoke must agree the same with his/ her line manager. Managers shall decide what is reasonable and operationally viable and may request that employees make up the time.
  7. Employees are not allowed to smoke inside the cars; whether private or company-owned; parked inside the premises of the Company or when driving within the site perimeters. Employees travelling in their own cars (outside the Company premises) with other employees on official business shall not smoke, unless they have agreed it with their colleagues.
  8. Managers/ supervisors are responsible for ensuring the implementation and maintenance of this Code. ‘No-smoking’ signs shall be clearly displayed at the entrances to and within the premises, and in all smoke-free vehicles of the Company.
  9. Failure to conform to the requirements of the Code by smoking in office premises or refusing to stop when requested shall constitute a disciplinary offence on behalf of the staff. At the first instance, a breach of the Code shall be dealt with an oral warning from the Head of HR, and a second infringement may result in formal disciplinary action.

14. Loans and other Credit Facilities from Financial Institutions

Any loan and/or credit facility obtained on the basis of false representations by the employee shall be deemed to be a violation of the Code.

15. Responding to Inquiries from the Press and Others

  1. Only authorized spokespersons shall speak with the press as the Company representatives.
  2. Requests for information about the Company from the media, the financial community, or the public shall be referred to the Head of HR.
  3. Requests for information from regulators or the government shall be referred to the Company Secretary.

16. Confidentiality

  1. The confidential information shall include all non-public information, correspondences, documents, papers, records, drawings, and data.
  2. Furthermore, employees are prohibited to share salary & other benefits information with anyone failing which, strict disciplinary action will be taken against the offender which includes but not limited to, withholding promotion/ increment, demotion and termination of services.
  3. The employees shall maintain the confidentiality of information entrusted to them or which comes to employee’s knowledge because of the position that the employee holds.
  4. An employee shall disclose confidential information, only if the employee is duly authorized by the Company or is legally mandated to do so. Prior to making a disclosure of any confidential information which is legally mandated, an employee shall be required to consult with the Head of HR.
  5. An employee shall not make or keep for personal use, copies of any confidential information. All confidential information shall be surrendered to the Company when a person ceases to be an employee.
  6. This responsibility continues in the case of ex-employees and the Company shall pursue all available legal remedies to prevent current and former employees from benefiting or misusing such confidential information.

17. Compliance with Law and the Code

  1. An employee shall be expected to comply, in both letter and spirit, with all applicable federal and provincial laws, rules and regulations along with the Code, and he/ she is to promptly report any actual or suspected violations to the HR Department.
  2. No one shall be subjected to retaliation for reporting a suspected violation. If an employee fails to comply with the Code or any applicable laws, rules or regulations, the employee shall be subject to disciplinary actions under the rules of the Company, including termination of employment.
  3. To avoid inadvertent violations, an employee shall be encouraged to ask questions to resolve any uncertainty as to whether some act or conduct complies with the Code. To encourage open communication, employees are advised to discuss the related matters with the Head of HR.

18. Rights Created

The Code shall be a statement of certain fundamental principles, policies and procedures that govern the Company employees.

19. Disclosure of Interest, Affiliation or Activity

  1. The following procedures shall be established to allow disclosure of any material interest, affiliation, or activity on the part of any employee that conflicts with, is likely to conflict with, or may appear to conflict with the duties of any employee, the Company, or the Code.
  2. On an annual basis, the HR department shall distribute to all staff members, a questionnaire to elicit disclosures of conflict or possible conflicts.
  3. The Human Resources department shall distribute to each newly hired employee, at the time of employment, a questionnaire to elicit disclosure of conflicts or possible conflicts. Any employee who, in the course of year, becomes aware of a potential conflict shall immediately contact the Head of HR to obtain and complete the questionnaire.
  4. All completed questionnaires shall be returned to the Head of HR, who shall prepare a report for determination of any act that indicates a possible conflict.
  5. All information reported by questionnaire shall be treated as confidential, except to the extent necessary for the protection of the Company interest or as required by any applicable law.
  6. Employees not subject to the disclosure procedures, as represented by completion of questionnaire, shall not be excluded from the Code. On an annual basis, the HR department shall distribute to every employee, the Code and a memo stating that the Code was previously signed by the employee and is still in full force and effect.
  7. The HR department shall obtain a signed Code from each new hired employee at the time of employment.

20. Sanction

Any breach of the requirement and procedure of the Company’s Code shall expose the employee to disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

21. Integrity

Integrity on the job means;

  1. Treating staff fairly;
  2. Comply with all laws, rules, regulations and policies that govern the conduct of the Company business, wherever that business is performed.
  3. Ensure that all transactions are handled honestly and recorded accurately.
  4. Avoid conflicts of interest, both real and perceived.
  5. Not to use the Company’s assets, information, or relationships for personal gain.
  6. Refrain from any acts of retribution or retaliation against an employee who has properly reported a business conduct issue or question.
  7. Respect the right of every employee to a fair treatment and an equal opportunity, free from harassment.
  8. Conduct all business dealings with honesty and fairness.

22. Policy Custodianship & Audit

  1. Policy audit will be conducted by Head of HR at least once a year.
  2. Head of HR will be the custodian of this policy and any clarities pertaining to this policy will be fetched from him while he will be the final authority in translating this policy.

Conflict of Interest Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Policy Details

In line with the IPAK Code of Conduct, whose spirit is to ensure principle-based business practices; IPAK defines the following as constituting a conflict of interest:

  1. Personally, engaging in business on behalf of the company with a business enterprise associated with anyone with whom there is a blood relation.
  2. Accepting gifts which are not nominal in nature, or a part of general promotion, from vendors, suppliers, customers. This may hamper professional discharge of duties.
  3. Accepting cash from vendors, suppliers, or customers.
  4. Using Company information available to employees for personal purposes, including financial gain
  5. Not declaring financial interest or gain in any business enterprise, which provides services, property, or goods to IPAK
  6. Not adhering to the Code of Conduct
  7. Not declaring the vested interest in a business enterprise which provided property, services, or goods to IPAK
  8. Not declaring the vested interest in a business enterprise which provided property, services, or goods to IPAK’s competitor
  9. Not declaring relatives working in the company in line with the “IPAK Anti Nepotism Policy”

Grievance Handling Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Definition of Grievance

Grievance represents a situation in which an employee feels that because of the conduct of another employee towards him/her, something unfavorable has happened or is going to happen to him/her. It can be any discontent or dissatisfaction, whether expressed or not, that an employee think believes or feels as unfair, unjust, or inequitable.

1.2. Privacy & Transparency

The Management will ensure that the privacy of the complainant at the maximum level. Further, transparency in grievance handling process shall be followed.

1.3. Grievance Reporting by the Employee

  1. If an employee wants to report his/her grievance against any other employee, he/she can approach any of the following persons and discuss about the issue (s) that he/she is facing:
    • Line Manager
    • Functional Director/Head
  2. If grievance is not addressed by the position mentioned above, employee can raise a “Employee Grievance Handling” ticket on IPAK ESS portal which would be directly reviewed and handled by Head of HR.
  3. If grievance is not resolved, CEO as final resort, can be approached with valid documentation.
  4. The above communication channels give the employee sufficient flexibility to report his/her grievance. Any undue escalation, beyond communication channels, may cast doubts on the intentions of the employee.

1.4. Grievance Handling by the Management

  1. When a grievance comes to the notice of the Management, then the complainant should be given a patient hearing and he/she should be allowed to express himself/herself completely.
  2. Authenticity of the facts should be examined to determine the legitimacy of the grievance.
  3. If the grievance of the complainant according to the initial assessment of the Management suggests misconduct, then the process defined in Disciplinary Matters Policy shall be followed.
  4. If the grievance is not related to an apparent misconduct, the Management should after consulting with the necessary stakeholders, resolve the issue.

Whistle Blowing Policy

1. Policy Guidelines

1.1. Policy Statement

“All employees are expected to maintain high standards of honesty and integrity, and are encouraged to report any wrongdoings observed”

1.2. Introduction

  1. International Packaging Films Limited (IPAK) is committed to operating practices of highest possible standards, carried out in an ethical, professional, and legal manner.
  2. This policy sets out the procedures for lodging of complaints and concerns by employees and the outside parties, handling of complaints by the Company, reporting requirements, actions, and periodic monitoring of the mechanism.

1.3. Objective

  1. Encourage all employees and outside parties to identify and report any improper conduct and wrongdoing within the Company.
  2. Provide avenues for confidentiality of concerns raised and allow recommendations on corrective measures.
  3. Provide protection to a whistleblower from possible reprisals or victimization.

1.4. Scope

The Policy covers following concerns:

  1. Conduct by anyone, which is an offence / breach of law.
  2. Failure to comply with legal obligations.
  3. Violation / non-compliance of Company’s policies and procedures.
  4. Health and safety risks, including risks to the public as well as employees.
  5. Damage to the environment.
  6. Damage to Company assets and corporate image.
  7. Possible fraud / corruption and incorrect reporting with mala fide intentions.
  8. Unauthorized use of sensitive company data.
  9. Actions which are unprofessional, inappropriate or unethical.
  10. The scope will also cover but not limited to; any breach of IPAK code of conduct policy as prescribed from time to time
  11. Practices or behaviours that compromise any material and product safety, legality, or quality throughout the process.
  12. Any actions undermining the organization’s commitment to continuous improvement and compliance with standards.

1.5. Definitions

  1. Company: IPAK
  2. Allegation: Accusation before proving with evidence.
  3. Improper conduct or Wrongdoing: Any fraudulent activity, theft, corruption, substantial mismanagement of Company’s resources, misuse of Company’s assets, breach of law, a civil wrong or criminal act, other violations of Company’s Code of Conduct / core values.
  4. Complaint: The information about any alleged fraud, improper conduct or wrongdoing. Also, any breach or violation including but not limited to; all prescribed in clause ‘6.4 – Scope’ of this policy.
  5. External Bodies: Any external person, group of persons and any state regulatory body.
  6. Victimization: Unwarranted singling out of an employee or group of employees for subjection to crime, exploitation, offense, and unfair treatment etc.
  7. Whistleblowing: Voluntary disclosure by an employee of a wrongdoing in the Company, to the whistleblowing committee.
  8. Whistleblower: Any employee who makes or attempts to make a disclosure of improper conduct, wrongdoing, or fraud to the whistleblowing committee.
  9. Whistleblowing Committee (WBC): The organizational body responsible for handling all concerns raised under the Whistleblowing Policy.

1.6. Confidentiality & Protection of Whistleblower

  1. IPAK encourages its staff to blow the whistle if they become aware of a malpractice that has occurred or is occurring. However, it is expected that the whistleblower will raise concerns in good faith, without any malice and in line of duty towards the Company. Further, there must be sufficient reason to believe that there is serious misconduct. Malicious allegations by employees may result in disciplinary action against the complainant.
  2. The identity of whistleblower will not be disclosed, and information passed on through whistleblowing will be kept confidential to protect the reporting person as well as the accused for any consequent discrimination or unfair treatment. The whistleblower will be protected against unfair treatment from his / her colleagues and management and any harassment or pressure towards the whistleblower will not be tolerated by the Company. Anonymous complaints, however, may not be entertained.
  3. WBC will not disclose internal concerns to any external body unless the concern raised involves legal provisions or the disclosure is required by law.

Disclosure to External Bodies

WBC shall not disclose internal concerns to any external body unless the concern raised involves legal provisions or the disclosure is required by law.

False Allegations

Malicious allegations by employees may result in disciplinary action against the complainant and penalties may be imposed.

1.7. Whistleblowing Committee

  1. The WBC shall assess the reasonableness / adequacy of such reporting and shall recommend investigation, if needed. CEO shall have the authority to decide final course of action after the investigation.
  2. If the complaint is against any member of the WBC, the whistleblower may report the matter to the CEO. The accused member of the WBC shall not be part of the committee for the concerned complaint.
  3. The WBC meeting shall include at least two of the following members, with presence of the Head of Internal Audit as compulsory:
    • Head of Internal Audit
    • Head of HR
    • Company Secretary
    • Head of Internal Audit shall be Chairperson of WBC
  4. The WBC shall:
    • Conduct an enquiry in a fair and unbiased manner.
    • Ensure complete fact finding.
    • Minute committee deliberations.
    • Document final report.
  5. Authority
    • All members of the WBC shall have access to the dedicated whistleblowing email address – refer Section 5 (emails coming to the whistleblowing email shall go automatically to all members of the committee).
    • The WBC is empowered to initiate enquiry against employee at any level and position. However, CEO shall be informed prior to instigate an enquiry against senior management level personnel.
    • The WBC shall have access to all records, documents and information necessary for its investigation. Employees shall facilitate WBC in its investigation and provide assistance, where required.

1.8. Procedure: How to Blow the Whistle

The complaint can be reported via following methods:

  • Through email at whistleblowing@ipak.com.pk preferably with whistleblower’s identity
  • A phone call to the Head of Internal Audit on 021-34384044-46-47-48 Ext. 12
  • Letter by post to Head of Internal Audit at the Karachi Head Office address: Plot# 40-L-1, P.E.C.H.S. Block Near Jason Trade Center, Karachi 75400, Pakistan
  • Each concern received by the WBC will be logged and acknowledged to the whistleblower.
  • WBC will then review and investigate the matter of concern. Expert opinion may be called whenever deemed necessary.
  • Depending on the nature and significance of complaint, the Board Audit Committee (BAC) may be consulted for guidance and recommended course of action.

1.9. Reporting

Log of whistleblowing complaints and status of proceedings shall be updated to BAC on quarterly basis by Head of Internal Audit.

The Terms of Reference of the BAC, as mentioned in Code of Corporate Governance Regulations of Pakistan (Section 27, para 4, clause xiv), include “review of arrangement for staff and management to report to audit committee in confidence, concerns, if any, about actual or potential improprieties in financial and other matters and recommend instituting remedial and mitigating measures.”

1.10. Action

Complaint Handling

Each complaint shall be assessed by the WBC with a view to decide whether it meets the criteria laid down in the policy, and whether a further investigation is required.

Disciplinary Action

If the results of an investigation confirm that an offence has been committed, depending on the severity of the offence / violation, the following sequence of actions may be suggested by WBC:

  • First warning: disciplinary warning, in writing
  • Second warning, in writing
  • Postponement or stoppage of increment or promotion
  • Recovery from the pay or otherwise of the whole or part of any pecuniary loss caused to the Company by the employee
  • Suspension without pay (as per regulations).
  • Termination
  1. The final investigation report shall be forwarded to HR department in case disciplinary action is required and the actions shall be taken in accordance with terms of employment contract and Company policies.
  2. If the magnitude of the complaint is material and the same has also been established by the WBC, then immediate suspension or termination can be suggested with the approval of CEO. In addition, the Company also reserves the right to initiate a legal proceeding against the culprit.
  3. Prior to initiate any above penal action a formal ‘Show-Cause Notice’ shall be issued to the accused / culprit by the HR Department on advice of WBC and with consent of the CEO; to provide him with a fair opportunity to clarify his position and actions in writing and/or through a hearing in person.
  4. Head of Internal Audit shall keep good records of the whistleblowing complaints, proceedings of Whistleblowing Committee (WBC) meetings, investigations and suggested actions to protect evidence, ensure credibility and avoid claims of discrimination.

1.11. Implementation

  1. The Whistleblowing Policy is to be placed at highly visible locations throughout the Company both in English and Urdu languages.
  2. Publishing the policy on Company’s website.
  3. Conducting training sessions on the policy as a refresher for employees and provide any clarifications needed.
  4. Policy audit will be conducted by WBC at least once a year.
  5. WBC will be the custodian of this policy and any clarities pertaining to this policy will be fetched from the WBC while the WBC will be final authority in translating this policy.
